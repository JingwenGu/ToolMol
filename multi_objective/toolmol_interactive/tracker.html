Strategies, and what they returned

120 episodes

Worked

  • Spending generation 1 on controlled probes instead of greedy edits. A generation is scored only after all ten offspring exist, so 50 calls buy three feedback rounds. Ten matched comparisons resolved more than ten opportunistic improvements would have.
  • Reusing the same host and anchor when the sampler returned a molecule already edited. Three run-1 episodes landed on one pyrazolyl-acetamide; grafting different motifs at the identical atom made those a controlled series rather than three unrelated attempts.
  • Building a control designed to fail. N-methylating the arylamine gave jnk3 0.00 against 0.10 for the otherwise identical molecule — proof the donor hydrogen is essential, at no Φ cost, because removing a donor raised QED to 0.896. The highest-information episode of run 1.
  • Deriving each parent's hidden jnk3 from its Φ. The prompt gives Φ, QED and SA, so activity follows by arithmetic. It is the only per-molecule activity signal available inside an episode.
  • Carrying the memory across runs. Run 2 reached run 1's final score on call 21 and finished +0.09 higher.
  • Rebuilding the known winner in generation 1. One call, and it scored identically to run 1 — confirming the oracle is deterministic and putting a Φ-2.21 parent in the population immediately, so generations 2 and 3 refined instead of searching.
  • The one-call pharmacophore graft. Anchoring on a peripheral atom whose own side is one carbon discards the entire host and installs a complete designed molecule in a single tool call. Run 1 needed two or three.
  • Systematic position scans. Mapping the pyridine ring, then the central ring, is what found the meta geometry — the largest single gain of run 2.
  • Deletion controls. Four calls across runs 1 and 3 — methylate each donor, remove the cap, delete the azine ring nitrogen — reduced three runs of scattered findings to one sentence: a free aryl N–H with a ring nitrogen ortho to it, and everything else is a modifier. None produced a good molecule; all four earned their call.
  • Re-opening a settled variable when the tweaks flatten out. Run 3 had four consecutive episodes land within 0.01 of each other. Going back to a position declared "insensitive" in run 2 and re-scanning it found bromine, worth +0.07 — the largest single gain of run 3.
  • Spending one probe per generation well outside the sampled range. Run 4's phenyl-at-C4 episode was framed as "find the size ceiling" and was expected to fail. It returned jnk3 0.70 against a band of 0.51–0.61 that ten substituents had occupied, and became the entire result of the run.

Did not work

  • Literature chemotype intuition. Indazole 0.02, carbazole 0.01, benzimidazole 0.03, benzothiazole 0.01 — and the textbook anilinoquinazoline 0.05. Roughly six of run 1's thirty episodes went into chemotypes that a fingerprint model simply does not reward.
  • Buying jnk3 with molecular weight. The highest activity of run 1's first generation, 0.10, came on a 413 Da molecule whose QED collapsed to 0.446, scoring Φ 1.404 — worse than a 241 Da molecule at jnk3 0.07 and Φ 1.829.
  • Generalising from a comparison made at low signal — three times. "Meta ≈ para" (measured at jnk3 0.10, wrong by 0.09), "keep the central ring bare" (generalised from a single fluorine), and "C4 is substituent-insensitive" (measured in a 0.41–0.43 band; at double the activity that position spreads 0.49 to 0.60). Each was wrong, each cost part of a run, and each failed the same way. A rule is only valid in the activity band it was measured in.
  • Generalising from one substituent. A fluorine on the central ring hurt, so run 1 wrote "keep it bare". A methyl there in fact helps the para core.
  • Assuming effects add. That same central methyl is +0.03 on the para core and −0.06 on the better meta core; the full four-position scan was below the bare ring everywhere. The best molecule of run 2 is the simplest one.
  • Anchoring edits on the wrong atom. Aromatic ring nitrogens and stereocentres both refuse re-attachment on valence grounds. Two offspring lost, one of them producing no molecule at all, before the mechanism was understood and written down.
  • The bis-arylamine. A second azine linkage raises jnk3 to 0.34 but costs 0.16 of QED — it buys activity at a worse exchange rate than the anilide it replaces.
  • Chasing small combinations once Φ plateaued. Mid run 2, four consecutive cap and C4 swaps all landed within 0.01 of each other. The plateau broke only when a structural variable — ring geometry — was changed instead.
  • Taking the familiar anchor out of habit. Several run-3 episodes anchored on a terminal methyl because it was the idiom I knew, which forces a methyl into the product — and methyl turned out to be the worst substituent at the one position that mattered. When the host carried a cycloalkyl, anchoring there instead would have left the choice free.
  • Looking for a better chemotype. Run 3 spent nine episodes on alternative azines and linkers. Every one lost: all four diazines, the sulfonamide, the reversed amide, the urea, the thioamide, and a second azine on the cap. Useful as a boundary, but it bought no molecules — the gain came from re-examining the chemotype already in hand.
  • Chasing one objective without checking the arithmetic on the other. Run 4's whole third generation went into making the aryl-at-C4 discovery affordable — eight polar heteroaryls replacing the phenyl. Activity held beautifully, up to 0.74. But every one of them landed at QED 0.71–0.75 regardless of polarity, because the penalty is the third aromatic ring itself, not lipophilicity. Ten episodes, +0.13 of activity, −0.14 of QED, and Φ did not move.
  • Believing a ceiling, four times. "meta ≈ para", "keep the central ring bare", "C4 is substituent-insensitive", and "C4 tops out at 0.61" — each was the edge of what had been sampled, not a limit.

Run 4 pushed activity furthest of any run — jnk3 0.74 — and gained almost nothing on Φ. That is the clearest result of the project so far: at the one position where activity can still be bought, the two objectives are in direct conflict, and the exchange rate is almost exactly 1:1. The open question for run 5 is narrow and concrete — whether any non-aromatic group delivers aryl-like bulk at that position. If none does, ~2.39 is this chemotype's ceiling under this scoring function, and saying so is the useful answer.
